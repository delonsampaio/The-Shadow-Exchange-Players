# The Downtime Phase

After every score, you enter Downtime. This is where characters breathe, recover, and pursue what matters to them outside the job. Don't skip it — it's half the game.

---

## The structure

Each PC gets **two free Downtime Activities**. Additional activities cost 1 Coin or 1 Rep each. Take them in whatever order makes sense for your character.

---

## The activities

### Indulge Vice
Go to your Vice Purveyor and indulge your vice. Clear Stress equal to your highest die result (roll dice equal to your **lowest** attribute rating). 

If you clear more Stress than you currently have, you **overindulge** — your purveyor gets in trouble, you cause a scene, or you lose something. Pick what the trouble looks like. It's a roleplay moment, not a punishment.

*One sentence is enough for the scene: "I spend the afternoon at Harlan's diner, three cups of coffee and a conversation I don't quite remember the end of."*

### Acquire Asset
Roll to acquire a temporary item or resource for the next score — special equipment, a hired hand, access to a specific location. Quality of what you can get scales with the crew's Tier (currently 0, so your options are modest).

### Long-Term Project
Work toward a personal goal that takes more than one session. You describe what you're doing and roll the relevant action; ticks on a clock track your progress. When the clock fills, the project completes.

Good first projects:
- Researching something unusual you encountered on the last score.
- Modifying a piece of Containment Suite gear.
- Mending a relationship with your Close Friend NPC.
- Following up on an entry in the Hunter's Journal.

*If you pick this activity in Session 1's downtime, you are starting a character throughline.*

### Recover
Seek out a physicker (a medic or healer) to begin clearing Harm. Roll the relevant action; ticks fill your healing clock. When the clock fills, your Harm drops by one level.

*You need this if you took Level 2 or 3 Harm during the score. Level 1 Harm can sometimes clear on its own.*

### Reduce Heat
*(**Heat** is how much attention the crew has drawn; high Heat can trigger an **Entanglement** — an unwanted consequence of a job.)* The crew generates Heat from how visible their operations are. Roll to lower the crew's Heat — cover your tracks, destroy evidence, bribe the right people, disappear from the right records.

*Worth doing when Heat is climbing. At high Heat, the Entanglement table starts producing real problems.*

### Train
Mark 1 XP in an action group or your playbook track. Describe briefly what your character was doing between sessions to develop that skill.

---

## Crew Advancement

At the end of each session, the GM reviews four XP triggers with the crew and marks XP for any that occurred. When the crew's track fills (8 XP), they advance — taking a new special ability or two crew upgrade boxes.

The crew's specific trigger for *The Shadow Exchange*: **completing a score that holds the cover-up together, or taking meaningful action to protect a Twice-Born from extraction or exposure.**

---

## Entanglements

If the crew's Heat is high enough after a score, roll for an Entanglement — an unwanted consequence from the job's footprint. This can be a Ministry visit, an angry contact, a rival firm making a move, or something worse. Low Heat in the early game means few Entanglements. Let Heat climb and they become a recurring problem.

---

## A note on Vice

Your character's Vice is not a character flaw to be optimized away. It is the thing your character reaches for when the work gets to be too much. Play the Vice scene. Give it texture. The campaign earns its weight in those scenes as much as in the scores.
