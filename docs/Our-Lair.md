# Our Lair — Curios & Chronicles

*What your characters know before the first session.*

---

## The Firm

**Curios & Chronicles** is a licensed monster-hunting firm in Old City. It has been operating for two years. You are the crew. Your front is a bookstore.

You are good at this in the way that people who do hard, quiet work for years get good at it — not the way an action-movie hero is good at it. You have stories you don't tell. You have a system. You have a cat named Consequence who has never once reacted to anything unusual, and you have all decided to find this reassuring.

---

## The Building

A narrow three-story brick building sandwiched between two glass-and-steel skyscrapers like a relic of a forgotten century. It used to be a savings bank. It shouldn't exist. The rent is paid through mechanisms none of you fully understand. It makes twenty dollars a day on purpose. That's the point.

### The Front Shop

To any passerby: a struggling bookstore. Old parchment, vanilla, floor wax. A rotary phone. A half-knitted sweater on the reading chair. A sleeping cat on a 1962 Sears catalog that has no business being there. The books are organized by a system only the crew understands.

In the back corner, near the Occult & Folklore section, behind a stack of moldering atlases: an old **Zoltar machine** — a glass-fronted booth the size of a phone box, a robed automaton inside with a painted beard and brass hands. The cards are long gone. The machine has been unplugged at the wall for decades.

You push a specific coin into the slot in a specific sequence — a code only you know — and Zoltar's glass eyes flicker once, green. His head turns one degree to the left. The wall behind him slides back with the heavy pneumatic hiss of a train door.

### The Containment Suite

Eleven iron steps down. The pneumatic door seals behind you. Vanilla and old parchment, replaced by something colder — a chemical tang, the sort of clean you only smell in dental offices and morgues. Fluorescent panels click on overhead, one by one.

**The Post-Op Table** — zinc-topped, surgical-grade, leather straps bolted to the corners. The zinc is scratched in patterns that suggest something was very interested in getting up. The straps have stains that didn't quite come out. This is where you open things, examine them, or coax them into giving up what's inside.

**The Scanner Station** — three modified shortwave radios on a rolling cart, tuned to different Ministry frequencies. They intercept bounty traffic before the public list updates. Beside them, an older fourth unit with a single green lamp — the Resonance detector, reach about two blocks — that has not been off in two years. On clear nights, between official broadcasts, you can pick up a voice on AM — it sounds like another state; no one has ever triangulated it — talking about things he shouldn't know. He is wrong about almost everything. He is never wrong about where.

**The Hunter's Journal** — an old leather-bound journal on a reading stand. Half the handwriting is yours. Half isn't. When you open it before a hunt, the people who came before you are still helping — some in pencil, some in pen, one of them in red ink, four years ago, in handwriting none of you recognize.

**Cold Storage Locker** — a bank of stainless steel drawers, each inscribed with silver runes. Pull one open and the cold air that spills out is sharp enough to fog. Things that should be spoiling don't. Months, sometimes, when the wall is sealed.

**Lead-Lined Vault** — set into the brick, heavy as a small car, a row of small mirrors sunk flush into the front face. When it's closed and sealed, what's inside goes quiet — stops humming, stops calling, stops appearing on any scanner. You have tested this. Ministry vehicles drive past. They find nothing. The mirrors are not for looking at.

The Suite smells different from the rest of the shop. Colder. Metallic. You have learned not to comment on it.

**The Trophy Wall** — a corkboard on the back wall, pinned with polaroids. Each one annotated in red marker: a date, a name, a cause of death that is not the cause of death the Ministry filed. Every hunt that has happened in this building's life is on that wall. The earliest photograph is older than one of you. None of you put it there.

---

### Upstairs and the Wall of Rules

**The Annex** (2nd floor): kitchenette, hot plate (tea is always on), three mismatched armchairs, and a whiteboard of *Things We Don't Say*.

**The Lofts** (3rd floor): three bunks, a clothes line, a footlocker each — and the shelf where your Cursed Heirloom lives.

On the plaster by the iron stair, in four different hands, is **the Wall of Rules**:

1. Nobody says "basement."
2. Cold things go in the cold place. *Today.*
3. If it hums, it goes in the vault.
4. If it sings, it goes in the vault *and you leave.*
5. Do not feed Consequence from the evidence.
6. *(in red ink, a fifth hand)* Do not trust anything that offers you tea.

*Nobody will tell you who wrote Rule 6.*

## The Work

Your firm is licensed by **The Ministry** — a government body that regulates the supernatural cover-up. They send jobs via damp manila envelope. Pay is minimal. They expect compliance and clean paperwork. You've learned to be creative about compliance.

The supernatural is real. The public doesn't know. Your job is to handle events and maintain the cover story. Exposure — video footage, credible witnesses, anything that reaches the press — ends your license and potentially your freedom.

**A witness with a phone is scarier than a wound.**

Some of what you handle are dangerous biological anomalies. Some are more complicated than that. Some of the things you've been contracted to neutralize are people — people who have found a way to exist in a world that doesn't know they exist. The firm keeps an **Anchor Ledger** — a record of every case and every choice made about what to do with what was found.

You also keep what you find. Monster parts, supernatural objects, things the Ministry says should be destroyed — you sell them instead, to a market the Ministry doesn't know exists. This is called the Shadow Exchange. This is the part that could get you killed.

---

## The Factions

**The Ministry (Tier V)** *(Tier = how big and powerful an organization is, from 0 to V; your crew starts at 0)* — Your license-holder. Bureaucratic, efficient, and not as monolithic as they present themselves. Your direct supervisor is Director Vance. He believes in procedure. He is not your friend. He is not your enemy. He is a process.

**Apex Aegis (Tier IV)** — A private firm that treats the supernatural as a resource extraction opportunity. Better equipment than you. Worse ethics. They are systematically acquiring or starving out every small licensed firm in the city. They have not approached you yet. When they do, it will not be a casual conversation.

**The Twice-Born** — People, for lack of a better word, who exist in both the human world and something older. Not a monolith. Not a threat, necessarily. Some of them are your best sources. Some of them are on your job roster. The distinction matters to you even when the paperwork doesn't make one.

## The Practical Stuff

- When the GM or Co-GM says "What does the crew do?" — that's your cue. Make a decision, individually or as a group.
- You can call a **Flashback** at any time to establish something your character prepared before the current scene. It costs Stress. Ask if you're not sure how it works.
- Your characters have worked in this building for two years. If you want to know whether you'd know something about the lair or its equipment — you would.
