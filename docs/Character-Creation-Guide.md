Build your character before Session 0. Send the GM what this guide asks for. We'll introduce everyone at the table and build the crew together.

---

## Who you are

You are not making "a character who hunts monsters." You are making **one of the people who run a small, underfunded monster-hunting firm called Curios & Chronicles, fronted by a bookstore in a city where the supernatural is real and the public doesn't know it.**

This is the second year of the firm's existence. Your character has already done some of this work. They have a system. They have stories they don't tell.

---

## Step 1 — Pick a playbook

Your playbook is your class. It gives you starting action dots, a list of special abilities to choose from, and a set of contacts (friends and rivals). You can take abilities from other playbooks later.

| Playbook | D&D analogue | Best for someone who wants to… |
|---|---|---|
| **Hound** | Ranger | Track targets, shoot precisely, operate tactically. |
| **Whisper** | Warlock / Wizard | Interact directly with the supernatural — Resonance (the faint signature supernatural things give off), entities, occult objects. |
| **Cutter** | Fighter / Barbarian | Be the physical threat in the room. *Note: Blades isn't HP attrition — pick Cutter because you like being the danger, not because you want to tank.* |
| **Lurk** | Rogue | Move unseen, bypass security, attack from hiding. |
| **Slide** | Bard / Face | Lie convincingly, manipulate officials, work social angles. |
| **Spider** | Mastermind / Inquisitive | Plan, read people, run schemes from a step back. **Strong choice for one player at the table — Spiders give the crew structure.** |
| **Leech** | Artificer | Tinker with gear, handle extractions and field surgery, improvise technical solutions. **Excellent fit for the Containment Suite** (the crew's hidden lab behind the bookshop). |

**Every playbook starts with 3 dots already filled in:** Cutter — Skirmish 2, Command 1 · Hound — Hunt 2, Survey 1 · Leech — Tinker 2, Wreck 1 · Lurk — Prowl 2, Finesse 1 · Slide — Sway 2, Consort 1 · Spider — Consort 2, Study 1 · Whisper — Attune 2, Study 1. *(Check against your playbook sheet if you have one.)*

> **Get your playbook sheet (free PDF):** see [Official Resources](Official-Resources.md) — one link per playbook. **Can't open it tonight?** No problem: pick your playbook, your Vice and your Heirloom, and **we'll choose your special ability and your Friend/Rival at Session 0 in two minutes.**

**For a first-time Blades table:** at least one Spider or Slide. Both have abilities that engage the crew's world politically and socially rather than just through dice. If everyone picks Cutters, the campaign will fight a lot and miss a lot.

If you're not sure what to pick, ask the GM. He knows what the rest of the table is building and can tell you what slot is open.

## Step 2 — Name, look, heritage, background, vice

- **Name & alias** — what you're called, and what you call yourself on the job.
- **Look** — three details. Reach for weather, posture, and a tell. ("Damp wool coat. Stooped. Never makes eye contact when answering directly.") Less is more.
- **Heritage** — where you're from. The campaign is a modern hidden-supernatural city. Pick a place that grounds your character. *(Ignore the heritage list printed on the official sheet — it's for a different city.)*
- **Background** — what you did before you found out monsters were real. Pick one of the standard backgrounds (*Academic, Labor, Law, Trade, Military, Noble, Underworld*) and write a one-line specific job. Not "law enforcement" — "forensic accountant for a firm that handled industrial insurance claims."
- **Vice & Purveyor** — what unhealthy thing you indulge to clear Stress, and the specific person or place who supplies it. Pick a vice you find interesting to *play*, not just one that sounds cool. You'll be returning to this every downtime. Options: *Faith, Gambling, Luxury, Obligation, Pleasure, Stupor, Weird.*

## Step 3 — Action ratings (your skills)

You have three **attributes** (Insight, Prowess, Resolve), each containing four **actions** — twelve skills total. Your **dots** in an action are how many six-sided dice you roll for that skill.

**How to place your 7 dots:**

1. **Your playbook starts with 3 dots already filled in** (see the table below Step 1).
2. **Add 4 more dots:** *one* in an action that reflects your **heritage**, *one* in an action that reflects your **background**, and *two* anywhere you like.
3. **Maximum 2 dots in any single action** at the start.

**How attribute dice work (important!):** when you **resist** a consequence or roll your **Vice** to clear Stress, you roll an *attribute* — and your attribute rating is **how many actions in that column have at least 1 dot** (0 to 4), **not** the total number of dots. Two actions at 1 dot = **2** attribute dice; one action at 2 dots = only **1**. So **spread your dots out** and put **at least one in each column** (Insight / Prowess / Resolve).

**Practical advice for ex-D&D players:**

- Don't optimize for one big number. Blades rewards *breadth* more than *spike*. A 1-dot action still rolls one die.
- Cover all three attributes (see "How attribute dice work" above). Resistance rolls are what keep you in the game.
- Not sure what an action covers? The [Quick Start](Quick-Start.md) lists all 12 in one line each; [Rules and Mechanics](Rules-and-Mechanics.md) has the long version.

## Step 4 — One starting special ability

Your playbook lists 8–9 special abilities. Pick **one** to start. You'll earn more through XP.

Rules of thumb:

- Read the ability twice. The first read is flavor; the second tells you when it actually triggers.
- Pick something that gives you a *new option*, not just a *bigger number*. An ability that lets you do something you otherwise couldn't is more interesting at the table than +1 die.
- Don't take a cross-playbook "veteran" ability at character creation. Save those for later.

---

## Step 5 — Close Friend, Rival

Your playbook lists contacts. **Pick one as a Close Friend** (someone who'll help you, with strings) and **one as a Rival** (someone who creates friction, with leverage).

Both will appear in play within the first three sessions. Tell the GM:

- Your Friend's name and one sentence on why this person owes you, or you owe them.
- Your Rival's name and one sentence on why the friction.

Make them **new people from your character's own life**. Director Vance (head of the Ministry's local office) is too senior to be your Friend or Rival, so keep any Ministry or Apex contact at clerk level. Harlan Okafor (see [Known Contacts](Known-Contacts.md)) works as a Friend but not as a Rival.

These are not optional flavor. They are how the GM knows which NPCs to put on stage in the opening months.

---

## Step 6 — Items & load

Don't build a packing list. In Blades, you choose what you brought *during the score* — your Load rating tells you how many slots you have. Look at your playbook's item list and mark the things your character gravitates toward. Those are your defaults; you'll choose specifics at the table.

---

## Step 7 — The Cursed Family Heirloom

This is a *Shadow Exchange* requirement, not standard Blades.

> **Answer this:** *What object did you carry into this life that you should have left behind?*

One sentence. Something concrete and specific — a pocket watch, a photograph, a piece of clothing, a journal in a language you don't speak. Something with weight from someone's life.

You don't need backstory for it now. Just the object. The GM will quietly do something with it as the campaign goes on.

---

## Step 8 — One question for the GM

Pick one and answer it in one sentence. Send it with your sheet.

- *What does your character think this work is for?*
- *Why is your character still doing this?*
- *What does your character want, in plain language, that this work might give them?*

Your answer becomes part of how the GM weights what happens to your character specifically.

---

## What to send the GM before Session 0

1. Your playbook choice.
2. Name (and alias), look (three details), heritage and background (one specific line), and your **Vice + Purveyor**.
3. Your action dot distribution (if you don't have your sheet, just say which actions you'd put your 4 extra dots in).
4. Your starting special ability.
5. Close Friend — name + one sentence.
6. Rival — name + one sentence.
7. Your Cursed Family Heirloom — the object.
8. Your answer to the one question.

That's enough. Everything else, we figure out at the table.

---

## Last notes

**You are not your character.** The system rewards taking damage, trauma, and complicated moral hits. Don't play to win — play to make the story interesting. Your character's worst moments are where the campaign earns its weight.

**You don't need to know the rules cold.** The GM and the AI Co-GM running sessions will explain every mechanic the first time it appears, in terms that make sense if you're coming from D&D. Your job is to know your character and make decisions that feel right for them.

**Ask questions.** Blades is full of small mechanics that look strange until they click. Ask before the session, ask during — there are no wrong questions.
