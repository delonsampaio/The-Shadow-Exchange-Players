# Welcome to The Shadow Exchange

> *"The world is exactly as it appears — if you're not looking closely. Governments function. Cities run. People go to work. And somewhere in the gap between a gas leak and a missing person report, something inhuman gets quietly erased from the record.*
>
> *That's the job. You are a small, underfunded monster-hunting firm. You take government contracts, you clean up the messes, and you make sure nobody sees what you saw. The Ministry calls you pest control. You let them.*
>
> *But you also keep what you find. Monster parts, supernatural items, things the Ministry says should be destroyed — you sell them instead, to a market the Ministry doesn't know exists. You are not the heroes the official story describes. You're something more interesting.*
>
> *Welcome to The Shadow Exchange."*

---

## What kind of game is this?

**Industrial-occult noir.** The supernatural is real. The public doesn't know. You are the people who clean it up — and quietly profit from it.

The tone is quiet dread and moral ambiguity, not action-movie heroics. The scariest thing in most scenes is a witness with a phone. Your clients are a government body that treats you as pest control. Your best leads come from a community of things the government is trying to exterminate.

You will make hard choices. The job will cost something. That's where the story lives — and between the hard choices, it is allowed to be very funny. Think monster-of-the-week: real dread, real heart, and a cat who is always asleep on the evidence.

---

## This is not D&D — here's what's different

| In D&D / Pathfinder | In *The Shadow Exchange* |
|---|---|
| Attack roll + damage roll | One roll. Most rolls produce both an outcome *and* a complication, even on a success. |
| HP attrition | **Stress** (push harder, resist consequences) and **Harm** (slow, accumulated damage). |
| Class features + spell slots | **Action ratings** (12 broad skills) and **special abilities** (playbook). |
| Plan before entering | **Engagement roll** drops you into the score already in progress. Plan retroactively via flashback. |
| GM rolls vs. you | The GM never rolls dice. Every roll is yours. |
| Loot is loot | Some loot has a name. A monster's tether to the human world is a person's most precious thing. Taking it has weight. |

The single biggest shift: **one roll produces a complete result**, including both what you accomplished and what it cost you. There is no "I rolled a 14 to persuade him" with no further consequence. Everything bites back a little.

## The shape of a session

Every session has the same rhythm:

1. **Free play** — brief scenes at the bookshop or in the city. 10–20 minutes. This is where your characters breathe.
2. **The score** — the job. You roll an Engagement Roll and you're already inside. 60–90 minutes.
3. **Downtime** — clear stress, pursue personal projects, deal with the aftermath. 30–45 minutes.

You will be in the middle of the score before you've finished planning. That's by design. If you needed to bring a specific tool or have a specific conversation first — you can flash back to having done it.

## Start here

**Never played Blades in the Dark? Perfect.** Three steps:

1. **Read the [Quick Start](Quick-Start.md)** — 5 minutes.
2. **Build your character** with the [Character Creation Guide](Character-Creation-Guide.md).
3. **Send the GM your character details** — the [Before Session 0](Before-Session-0.md) checklist has the exact list. *(Free playbook sheets: [Official Resources](Official-Resources.md).)*

That's it. Everything else is optional reference you can skim whenever you're curious: [What Kind of Game Is This](What-Kind-of-Game.md) · [Our Lair](Our-Lair.md) · [Known Contacts](Known-Contacts.md) · [Rules and Mechanics](Rules-and-Mechanics.md) · [Downtime Guide](Downtime-Guide.md) · [Glossary](Glossary.md) · [FAQ](FAQ.md) · [Session 0 Agenda](Session-0-Agenda.md).

You don't need to know the rules cold. The GM (and the AI Co-GM running sessions with us) will explain every mechanic the first time it comes up. What you need to know is your character and your vice.
