# Rules & Mechanics

*New to Blades? Read [Quick Start](Quick-Start.md) first — this page is the longer reference. Read it before distributing your action dots during character creation: you need to know what each action does before you decide where to put your points.*

---

## The Core Loop

**Engagement Roll → Score (action rolls, consequences, resistance) → Downtime → next score.**

You will be in the middle of the job before you've finished planning. That's the design. If you needed something — a forged badge, a bribed contact, a scouted route — you **flash back** to having arranged it before the scene started.

---

## Action Rolls

When you do something risky or uncertain, you roll a number of **d6** equal to your action rating. Take the highest die.

| Highest die | Result |
|---|---|
| **6** | Full success. You do it. |
| **4–5** | Mixed success. You do it, but there's a cost or complication. |
| **1–3** | Bad outcome. Consequences, and you may not succeed. |
| **Two 6s** | Critical. You do it, plus something extra goes right. |

Before every roll, the GM sets two things out loud:

- **Position** — how bad the consequences are if things go wrong: Controlled / Risky / Desperate. *(Rolling a **Desperate** action marks 1 XP in that action's attribute — win or lose.)*
- **Effect** — how much you can accomplish if you succeed: Limited / Standard / Great.

You can negotiate these. Saying "I take it slow and careful" might shift from Risky to Controlled but drop your Effect from Standard to Limited. The GM will always tell you before you roll.

## The 12 Actions

Your character has 12 action ratings — dots from 0 to 4. When you roll, you build a dice pool equal to your rating. Zero dots means you roll 2 dice and take the *lowest* (called a zero-die roll).

Distribute your dots knowing what each action covers:

### Insight — the thinking attribute

**Hunt** — carefully track a target. Follow someone, discover their location, set up an ambush, attack with precise ranged fire. *This campaign: identifying a Twice-Born by behavioral patterns, timing an extraction around an Apex Aegis patrol.*

**Study** — scrutinize details and interpret evidence. Research documents and books, recall specialist knowledge, detect lies or true feelings by close observation. *This campaign: identifying an entity type from physical evidence, reading Ministry paperwork for anomalies.*

**Survey** — observe the situation and anticipate outcomes. Spot trouble before it arrives, read a room for exits and threats, detect someone's intentions from their body language. *This campaign: reading how close a Twice-Born is to an episode, anticipating when the Ministry scanner van will pass.*

**Tinker** — fiddle with devices and mechanisms. Pick a lock, crack a safe, disable an alarm, build or modify gear, perform careful physical work on specimens. *This campaign: operating the Scanner Station, extracting a biological sample cleanly, modifying a containment case.*

> **Hunt vs. Survey:** Hunt focuses on a *specific target*. Survey reads an *environment or situation*.

### Prowess — the body attribute

**Finesse** — dextrous manipulation or subtle misdirection. Pickpocket, handle a vehicle precisely, fight with technique in a controlled situation, pass something off undetected. *This campaign: palming an Anchor Item in front of its owner, slipping a forged document into a courier's bag.*

**Prowl** — traverse skillfully and quietly. Sneak past a guard, move through a building without leaving traces, attack from concealment, cross difficult terrain undetected. *This campaign: entering a target's home without triggering an Apex scanner, tailing a subject without losing them.*

**Skirmish** — entangle a target in close combat. Brawl, wrestle, fight with weapons at close range, hold a position under pressure. *This campaign: subduing a Twice-Born mid-episode without killing them, holding a door against something trying to get through it.*

**Wreck** — unleash savage force. Smash through an obstacle, destroy something deliberately, force a breach, create chaos. *This campaign: forcing a welded-shut cold storage unit, destroying surveillance equipment on the way out.*

> **Skirmish vs. Finesse:** Skirmish is messy and committed. Finesse is precise and controlled.

### Resolve — the will attribute

**Attune** — open your mind to the supernatural. Perceive Resonance, communicate with or sense entities, read the nature of an occult object. *This campaign: reading an Anchor Item's Resonance signature to identify its owner, detecting whether a Twice-Born is present in a space.*

**Command** — compel swift obedience. Intimidate, issue orders that get followed, lead under pressure, establish authority through presence. *This campaign: getting a Twice-Born to stop mid-episode without a fight, holding the crew together when something goes sideways.*

**Consort** — socialize with friends and contacts. Build rapport, access resources or people through relationships, make connections through shared background. *This campaign: getting information out of a contact at the diner, building standing with a market vendor.*

**Sway** — influence with guile, charm, or argument. Lie convincingly, persuade, manipulate, argue someone into or out of a position using social pressure rather than relationship. *This campaign: talking past a Ministry inspector, convincing a witness they saw a gas leak.*

> **Consort vs. Sway:** Consort works through relationship — they like or trust you. Sway works through argument or pressure regardless of whether they like you.
>
> **Command vs. Sway:** Command uses authority or fear. Sway uses persuasion or manipulation.

## Stress

Stress is your resource. Spend it to push yourself harder or absorb consequences.

- **Push yourself** — +1 die to a roll, or improve your Effect by one level. Cost: 2 Stress.
- **Assist** another PC — +1 die to their roll. Cost: 1 Stress.
- **Resist a consequence** — roll your relevant attribute (Insight / Prowess / Resolve), spend (6 minus highest die) Stress to reduce or negate the consequence. *Resistance always works.*
- **Flashback** — establish something you prepared before the scene. Cost: 0 / 1 / 2 Stress depending on how plausible and complex it was.

*(Your **attribute** rating — Insight, Prowess or Resolve — is the number of actions in that column that have at least 1 dot. You roll it for resistance and for your Vice.)*

Max stress: **9.** When you hit 9, you take a **Trauma** and reset to 0.

---

## Resistance — It Always Works

When a consequence lands, you can resist it. You don't need to ask permission. Just say "I resist that."

- Roll your attribute (Insight, Prowess, or Resolve) — the GM picks which, based on the nature of the consequence.
- Spend Stress equal to **6 minus your highest die**. On a 6: 0 Stress. On a critical: clear 1 Stress instead.
- The consequence is reduced or negated. You may only resist each consequence once.

**The GM will prompt you in early sessions.** Don't accept a consequence you could have resisted three rolls ago.

---

## Harm

When you take physical or mental damage, you mark harm by level. Harm is slow — it builds up over time and needs active recovery.

| Level | Effect | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Lesser** | Reduced Effect on relevant rolls | Battered, Drained, Distracted, Scared |
| **2 — Moderate** | −1 die on relevant rolls | Exhausted, Deep Cut, Concussion, Panicked |
| **3 — Severe** | Can't act without help from another PC | Broken Leg, Shot, Badly Burned, Terrified |
| **4 — Fatal** | Out of action | Electrocuted, Stabbed in the Heart |

Resistance can reduce harm by one level *before* it's recorded. If you resist a Level 3 consequence, it becomes Level 2.

## Flashbacks — Planning in Reverse

You realize mid-score that you'd want a specific tool, an alibi, a contact, a key. You can have *already arranged it*:

- *"I flash back to having a forged Ministry badge on me."*
- *"I flash back to talking to our contact about the building layout last week."*
- *"I flash back to having modified the scanner before we left."*

Cost depends on how plausible and risky the prep was:
- **0 Stress** — something obviously plausible you'd have done with no difficulty.
- **1 Stress** — meaningful prep that had some risk or effort.
- **2 Stress** — complex or risky setup. The flashback may require a roll.

Flashbacks don't undo consequences already in play. They add new fiction that helps with what comes next.

## The Devil's Bargain

Before any roll, the GM can offer: "+1 die — but a complication happens regardless." The guard notices something. A scanner pings. Something breaks. The complication is real whether you succeed or fail. You can always say no to the bargain.

---

## Teamwork

**Assist:** Describe how you help and take 1 Stress. The other PC gets +1 die. No roll required.

**Group Action:** When the whole crew needs to do something together (group stealth, a simultaneous breach), everyone rolls. The single highest result counts for the group. The leading PC takes 1 Stress for each teammate who rolled 1–3.

---

## Combat — No Initiative

When violence breaks out, there are no turns. Combat is an obstacle like any other — an action roll with position and effect. We move the spotlight around the table cinematically. There is no hit point track to chip away. The fight ends when one side can no longer continue, not when a number hits zero.

---

## Load — The Quantum Inventory

You don't write out every item before a score. You declare your **Load** before the Engagement Roll:

| Load | Slots | What it signals |
|---|---|---|
| **Light — 3** | 3 items | Inconspicuous. Civilian. Fast. |
| **Normal — 5** | 5 items | Professional. Will draw stares in civilian areas. |
| **Heavy — 6** | 6 items | Slow. Loud. You look like you're going to work. |

During the score, when you need something, you declare you have it and check off a slot. The item was always there — you're deciding now what you brought.
